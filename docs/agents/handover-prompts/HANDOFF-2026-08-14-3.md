## Work Queue (in this order)

### A. Q91 — BYO-HttpClient surface: industry research, then seal (FIRST)

Blocker #1 (default `Authorization` on an injected client leaks into requests the docs
call anonymous) exposed the cost side of the public `(HttpClient, options)` constructor.
**Deliberately not sealed** — research first.

- **Research (fresh evidence, not recollection):** how Azure.Core/System.ClientModel,
  OpenAI, AWS, Stripe.net, Octokit, and the typed-client ecosystem treat caller-supplied
  transport: public BYO constructor or not; documented default-header semantics (whose
  headers win); how no-DI consumers get proxy/TLS/custom handlers; whether any SDK
  restricts transport injection to its DI companion package.
- **Recorded leaning (maintainer + session, 2026-08-14): internalize.** Make the
  `(HttpClient, options)` constructor `internal` + `InternalsVisibleTo("OpenCode.Sdk.Extensions")`
  (Tests/Performance IVTs already exist), leaving one public constructor; the Extensions
  `IHttpClientBuilder` becomes the sanctioned transport-composition seam; future no-DI
  needs (proxy et al.) are met additively via concrete options knobs — never a raw
  handler pass-through.
- **Acceptance bar if BYO stays public:** a first-class but pragmatic — not
  overengineered — resolution of the default-header ambiguity: at minimum a
  construction-time fail-closed guard (`Password == null` while the injected client's
  `DefaultRequestHeaders` carry `Authorization` → refuse) plus docs stating whose
  headers win. The proxy/TLS advantage of BYO is real; do not discard it silently.
- Batch the research table + recommendation to the maintainer as plain text; **seal
  before touching #1**.
